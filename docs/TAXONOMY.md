## D-FND — Legal System, Sources and Method

_Status: active._ The architecture within which every other discipline operates: the plural sources of Sri Lankan law, the court hierarchy and doctrine of precedent, and the techniques of statutory and constitutional interpretation. Not in the original scope list; added because Public Law cannot be learned coherently without it.

### S-FND-01 — Sources of Sri Lankan Law and Legal Pluralism

**Tier 1** — Foundational to every discipline; determines which rule applies to a dispute at all.

- **What it means:** The layered sources of law: the Constitution; Acts and subordinate legislation; Roman-Dutch law as the residuary common law; English law received by statute (e.g. commercial matters) and by judicial borrowing; personal/customary laws (Kandyan, Thesawalamai, Muslim law); judicial precedent; and, through incorporation, international law.
- **Why it matters:** Choice-of-law questions (which personal law, whether Roman-Dutch or English law governs) are outcome-determinative and recur in property, family, contract and delict litigation.
- **Historical origins:** Dutch administration of the maritime provinces (1658-1796) left Roman-Dutch law; British rule (1796/1815-1948) preserved it while introducing English law in commerce, procedure and evidence, and codifying Kandyan, Tamil (Thesawalamai) and Muslim customs.
- **Doctrinal foundations:** Continuance of existing law (Art 168, Art 16); Civil Law Ordinance 1852; personal-law statutes.
- **Practical application:** Every pleading implicitly asserts a governing law; personal-law applicability is pleaded and proved.
- **Legislative basis:** `CP-ART-168` Art 168 - Continuance of existing laws (unverified); `CP-ART-016` Art 16 - Existing written and unwritten law valid notwithstanding inconsistency with Chapter III (unverified); `ACT-CIVIL-LAW-1852` Civil Law Ordinance, No. 5 of 1852 (introduction of English law in maritime and commercial matters) (unverified); `ACT-KANDYAN-1938` Kandyan Law Declaration and Amendment Ordinance, No. 39 of 1938 (unverified); `ACT-THESAWALAMAI-1806` Thesawalamai Code (Regulation of 1806) and Jaffna Matrimonial Rights and Inheritance Ordinance, No. 1 of 1911 (unverified); `ACT-MMDA-1951` Muslim Marriage and Divorce Act, No. 13 of 1951 ✓

### S-CON-02 — Sovereignty, Constitutional Supremacy and Constitutional Amendment

**Tier 1** — Defines the legal limits on Parliament and the method of constitutional change; underpins all constitutional review.

- **What it means:** Sovereignty resides in the People (Art 3) and is exercised through organs of government (Art 4). The Constitution is supreme, but judicial control of legislation is only pre-enactment (Arts 120-124); once certified, an Act cannot be questioned (Art 80(3)). Amendment requires a 2/3 majority (Art 82), plus a referendum for entrenched provisions (Art 83).
- **Why it matters:** Sri Lanka is a hybrid: textual supremacy without post-enactment review. Bill challenges are the central constitutional litigation.
- **Historical origins:** 1972 Constitution gave Constitutional Court limited pre-enactment review; 1978 transferred this to SC. Doctrine on Arts 3/4 developed in Re 13A (1987) and Re 19A (2002).
- **Doctrinal foundations:** Arts 3, 4, 80(3), 82-84, 120-124.
- **Practical application:** Any citizen may petition the SC within a short window after a Bill is placed on the Order Paper; SC determination states majority required (simple, 2/3, or 2/3 plus referendum) and amendments that would cure inconsistency.

### S-CON-04 — Fundamental Rights and Constitutional Remedies

**Tier 1** — Most litigated constitutional jurisdiction; Art 12(1) also functions as Sri Lanka's principal vehicle of administrative law review.

- **What it means:** Chapter III rights (Arts 10-14A), restrictions (Art 15), and the Supreme Court's sole and exclusive jurisdiction under Art 126 against infringement or imminent infringement by executive or administrative action, with relief that is just and equitable.
- **Why it matters:** Direct, speedy, apex-court remedy; source of the right-to-life, public trust, legitimate expectation and arbitrariness doctrines in Sri Lanka.
- **Historical origins:** 1972 Constitution had rights without effective remedy; 1978 created Art 126; jurisprudence expanded in the 1980s-2000s (state action, standing, compensation); Art 14A added 2015.

## D-ADM — Administrative Law

_Status: active._ Judicial and non-judicial control of public power. A distinctive Sri Lankan feature is the dual track: writ review in the Court of Appeal (Art 140) and Provincial High Courts (Art 154P(4)), and rights-based review in the Supreme Court through Art 12(1) under Art 126.

### S-ADM-01 — Foundations of Judicial Review and the Rule of Law

**Tier 1** — Conceptual base for all review; links constitutional structure to administrative remedies.

- **What it means:** Public power exists only within legal limits; courts review legality, not merits. In Sri Lanka, review is grounded in the Constitution (Arts 3, 4, 12, 126, 140) and the received English common law of judicial review.
- **Why it matters:** Defines when a decision can be challenged at all and on what conception of power (public trust).
- **Historical origins:** Inherited English prerogative writs (Courts Ordinance / Charter of Justice); constitutionalised in 1978; public trust doctrine developed from the 1990s.
- **Doctrinal foundations:** Rule of law; no unfettered discretion; public trust; sovereignty of the People.
- **Practical application:** Framing a challenge: public-law character of the decision, forum, grounds, remedy.
- **Legislative basis:** `CP-ART-140` Art 140 - Writ jurisdiction of the Court of Appeal (certiorari, prohibition, mandamus, procedendo, quo warranto) (unverified); `CP-ART-126` Art 126 - Sole and exclusive FR jurisdiction of the SC; executive or administrative action; one-month time limit; just and equitable relief ✓; `CP-ART-003` Art 3 - Sovereignty is in the People and is inalienable (powers of government, fundamental rights, franchise) (unverified); `CP-ART-004` Art 4 - Manner of exercise of sovereignty (legislative, executive, judicial power; fundamental rights; franchise) (unverified)
